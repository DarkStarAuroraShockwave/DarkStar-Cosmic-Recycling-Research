# DarkStar — Cosmic Recycling Research

**Author:** Scott Hunter Hughes “DarkStar Aurora Shockwave”  
**Research status:** doctoral research draft / hypothesis program  
**Repository snapshot:** October 1, 2026

## Overview

DarkStar is a research program investigating black holes, radiation, mass-energy transformation, entropy, astrophysical outflows, and the possibility of cosmic recycling. The repository preserves the mathematical development, stress tests, dynamical models, toy tests, scientific audit, and integrated dissertation draft.

## Scientific status

This repository deliberately distinguishes:

- **Established physics** — standard results and observations used as foundations.
- **Derived/recast mathematics** — consequences or reformulations of established equations.
- **DarkStar model elements** — bookkeeping, parameterizations, and test structures introduced for this research.
- **Hypotheses** — proposed mechanisms that remain unestablished and require evidence.
- **Research frontiers** — unresolved mathematical or physical problems identified for future work.

The repository does **not** represent peer-reviewed confirmation of a new physical theory, and publication or repository hosting does not constitute endorsement by NASA or any other institution.

## Repository structure

- `docs/` — dissertation drafts, historical research documents, and source manuscripts.
- `models/` — mathematical and dynamical model development.
- `tests/` — stress tests, toy dynamical test, cosmic-bounce test, and scientific audit.
- `visuals/` — model/test visualizations.

## Mathematical development

The current research record includes the compactness benchmark `R_s = 2GE/c⁴`, the associated spherical energy threshold and energy-density benchmark, input-output accounting, dynamical state variables, a recycling fraction test, entropy constraints, and a Friedmann-based cosmic-bounce test. These are documented with their scientific status in the research files rather than being presented uniformly as new physical laws.

## Reproducibility and next work

The next scientific stage is to replace bookkeeping assumptions with explicit stress-energy models, spacetime geometry, matching/causal conditions where required, observational discriminators, and quantitative null tests against established astrophysical and cosmological models.
